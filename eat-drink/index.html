Eat and drink in Vlora

Three real places to start, in three parts of town — then what to order and how restaurants work.

Three places to start

Three different kinds of meal in three parts of town. Each comes from the restaurant’s own published details, checked on the date shown; we have not rated the food, nobody paid to be here, and the order is simply north to south.

Taverna Te Lilo

Editorial

By the port, near Independence Square (Sheshi i Pavarësisë)

A taverna whose menu is built around traditional Albanian dishes, with seafood, pasta and risotto alongside — and breakfast from the morning.

Good for
Traditional Albanian cooking in the centre, a short walk from the port and the north end of the Lungomare.
Keep in mind
There is no booking page — just a digital menu and phone numbers — so call ahead for a table in July and August.

Paradise Beach Restaurant

Editorial

Lungomare (Rruga Murat Tërbaçi), on the beach

A Mediterranean and Italian restaurant on the beach side of the Lungomare; the menu centres on local seafood, with a raw-fish bar and dry-aged steaks.

Good for
Tables by the sea on the main promenade — a sunset dinner within walking distance of most central hotels.
Keep in mind
It is on the busiest stretch of the promenade, so lively rather than quiet; reserve ahead for a sunset table in high summer.

Eden by Valza

Editorial

Uji i Ftohtë, on the coast road south of the centre

The rooftop restaurant of the Valza Boutique Hotel at Uji i Ftohtë: modern Albanian and Mediterranean cooking and Albanian wines, looking out over the bay.

Good for
A sit-down evening meal with a wide view across the bay towards the sunset, and Albanian wines on the list.
Keep in mind
It is an evening restaurant a taxi ride south of the centre, not somewhere to drop into from the promenade; book ahead.

What to eat

  • Fish and seafood

    Grilled whole fish, octopus, squid and prawns are the Vlora order. Fish is often priced by weight: ask to see it and confirm the price before it goes on the grill.

  • Vlora Bay mussels

    The bay is an important mussel-farming area, according to the National Tourism Agency — so mussels here travel a very short way to the plate.

  • Dukat olive oil

    The olive groves of the Dukat plain, south of the city, are the area’s oil country. Good on everything; good to take home.

  • Narta wine

    The village of Narta, by the lagoon north of Vlora, makes wine from the local Vlosh grape — ask for it by name.

  • Albanian classics

    Byrek (savoury pastry), tavë kosi (baked lamb with yoghurt), fërgesë (peppers, tomato and cottage cheese), qofte (grilled meatballs), stuffed peppers, and salads with white cheese.

  • Coffee and sweets

    Espresso is a ritual; Turkish coffee survives in older cafés. For dessert: baklava and trileçe, the three-milk cake.

Where the food is

The Lungomare
Restaurants, cafés and ice-cream all along the promenade south of the port — the busiest, easiest choice, and the place to be at sunset.
The centre and the port
Cafés around Flag Square and the streets inland, tavernas near Independence Square by the port, and bakeries for byrek in the morning.
Uji i Ftohtë and the coast road
South of the Lungomare, restaurants on terraces above the coves look back across the bay. A taxi ride from the centre.
Radhimë and Orikum
Beach restaurants and bars at the south end of the bay, busiest in season.
Dukat and Llogara
Farm lunches in olive country and mountain restaurants near the pass, on the way to the Riviera.

Cafés, beach bars and evenings

Vlora runs on coffee: cafés fill from early morning, and an espresso is a reason to sit down, not a takeaway. Beach bars open with the season — roughly late spring to early autumn — and the evening belongs to the xhiro, the slow walk along the Lungomare before a late dinner. In summer the waterfront often has open-air music; see What’s on for anything we can confirm.

How it works

  • Prices are in lek. Larger restaurants take cards; beach bars and small places often prefer cash.
  • Fish by weight: agree the price of the fish before it is cooked.
  • Tipping is appreciated, not expected — around ten per cent for good service.
  • Water: order bottled; UK advice is not to drink the tap water.
  • The bill: “Llogarinë, ju lutem” — the bill, please.
