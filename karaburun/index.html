The Karaburun Peninsula

The roadless mountain arm across the bay: cliffs, sea caves, coves you reach only by boat, ancient sailors’ inscriptions — and the dark ridge behind every sunset on the camera.

What it is

Karaburun is the long, mountainous peninsula that forms the western side of Vlora Bay — the ridge you see across the water from the Lungomare. It has no road along its coast and almost no inhabitants. Its outer side faces the open Ionian Sea; its inner side shelters the bay.

The sea around it belongs to the Karaburun-Sazan National Marine Park, declared in 2010. At its southern foot lie Orikum and the Pashaliman naval base, which is closed to visitors.

What to see

  • The Haxhi Ali cave — a sea-carved cave near the northern tip, the park’s best-known sight. Boats go in only when the swell allows.
  • Grama Bay — a small bay on the outer coast whose cliffs carry inscriptions in Ancient Greek, Latin and Medieval Greek, left by sailors giving thanks for a safe passage. Its sea caves are a refuge for the Mediterranean monk seal.
  • Coves and clear water all along the inner coast — the swim and snorkel stops of most boat trips.

Getting there

By boat is the normal way: shared trips and private charters from the Vlora waterfront, and boats from Orikum, which is closer. See two ways to go by boat.

Hiking
Parts of Karaburun are reached on foot by experienced hikers, but the peninsula is remote, with no services, no road access along the coast and very little shade. Do not attempt it without local knowledge or a guide. We will add trail notes only when we can describe them first-hand.
