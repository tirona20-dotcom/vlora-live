How to read the live view

Vlora Bay Webcam Guide

Recognise the landmarks, the activity and the changing conditions visible from the 24/7 camera overlooking Vlora Bay on Albania’s Adriatic coast.

Open the live camera

What the camera shows

Vlora Bay Livecam is a moving PTZ camera rather than one fixed view. The stream changes angle during the day to show a mix of wide bay scenery, the Lungomare and nearby beaches, port and ferry activity, boats, sunsets, city lights and occasional paragliders. The exact frame therefore changes, but the landmarks below help identify what is on screen.

Lungomare and the waterfront

The Lungomare is the long waterfront promenade visible in several closer camera angles. Depending on the preset, the scene can include palm-lined sections, beaches, the pier, the Ferris wheel, cafés and people using the waterfront. These views are usually most active during daylight and summer evenings.

Sazan Island

Sazan Island appears offshore in the wider views across the bay. Its outline is especially clear when visibility is good and often becomes a strong silhouette at sunset. Haze, cloud and changing light can make it appear sharper or more distant from one day to the next.

Karaburun Peninsula

The Karaburun Peninsula forms the mountainous western background of the bay. It gives the live view depth and frames the entrance to the bay. Clear days reveal ridge detail, while evening light turns it into a dark outline behind the water.

Port, ferries and boats

Ferries, working vessels, yachts and smaller boats appear in the bay and around the port approaches. Activity varies by time, weather and season. A vessel may cross a wide preset quickly, while closer views hold it longer when the camera is already facing the relevant area.

Paragliders above the bay

Paragliders occasionally cross the wide bay view when conditions are suitable. They can appear high above the shoreline and may remain visible across several camera angles before leaving the frame.

Beaches and summer activity

Closer coastal presets show beaches, swimmers and daily shoreline activity. These views are strongest in clear daylight, when the water colour and movement along the beach are easiest to see.

Best times to watch

Daylight. Daytime is best for identifying beaches, boats, ferries, the shoreline and distant landmarks. Visibility can vary considerably with haze and cloud.

Late afternoon and sunset. Golden hour and sunset often produce the strongest colour and contrast across the bay, particularly toward Sazan Island and the western horizon. Today’s sunset time is on the Vlora Today page.

Night. At night the most useful views are the city lights, the waterfront and illuminated harbour activity. Open sea views become too dark, so the camera prioritises scenes with visible detail.

Weather and visibility

The camera provides a real-time visual check of clouds, rain, wind effects on the water, sea colour and visibility. It is useful for seeing current conditions, but it is not a substitute for an official marine or weather forecast and should never be used as a safety assessment.

Why the view moves

Only one rooftop camera is used. It rotates between selected views so the stream can show more of the bay than a fixed webcam. The long-term aim is intelligent camera direction that stays longer on strong scenes and leaves weak framing quickly.
