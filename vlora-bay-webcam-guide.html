Vlora Bay Webcam Guide

Use this guide to recognise the main landmarks, activity and changing conditions visible from the 24/7 live camera overlooking Vlora Bay on Albania’s Adriatic coast.

What the camera shows

Vlora Bay Livecam is a moving PTZ camera rather than one fixed view. The stream changes angle during the day to show a stronger mix of wide bay scenery, Lungomare and nearby beaches, port and ferry activity, boats, sunsets, city lights and occasional paragliders. The exact frame therefore changes, but the main landmarks below help identify what is on screen.

Lungomare and the waterfront

Lungomare is the long waterfront area visible in several closer camera angles. Depending on the preset, the scene can include palm-lined sections, beaches, buildings, cafés, vehicles and people using the waterfront. These views are usually most active during daylight and summer evenings.

Sazan Island

Sazan Island appears offshore in the wider views across the bay. Its outline is especially clear when visibility is good and often becomes a strong silhouette during sunset. Haze, cloud and changing light can make it appear sharper or more distant from one day to the next.

Karaburun Peninsula

The Karaburun Peninsula forms part of the wider coastal background. It gives the live view depth and helps frame the entrance to Vlora Bay. Clear days reveal more detail, while evening light can turn the ridgeline into a dark outline behind the water.

Port, ferries and boats

Ferries, working vessels, yachts and smaller boats can appear in the bay and around the port approaches. Activity varies by time, weather and season. A vessel may cross a wide preset quickly, while closer views can hold it longer when the camera is already facing the relevant area.

Paragliders above the bay

Paragliders occasionally cross the wide bay view when conditions are suitable. They can appear high above the shoreline and may remain visible across several camera angles before leaving the frame.

Beaches and summer activity

Closer coastal presets show beaches, swimmers and daily shoreline activity. These views are strongest in clear daylight, when the water colour and movement along the beach are easiest to see.

Best times to watch

Daylight

Daytime is best for identifying beaches, boats, ferries, the shoreline and distant landmarks. Visibility can vary considerably with haze and cloud.

Late afternoon and sunset

Golden hour and sunset often produce the strongest colour and contrast across the bay, particularly toward Sazan Island and the western horizon. The exact sunset view depends on the camera angle and current conditions.

Night

At night, the most useful views are usually city lights, the waterfront and illuminated harbour activity. Open sea views can become too dark, so the camera prioritises scenes with visible detail.

Weather and visibility

The camera provides a real-time visual check of clouds, rain, wind effects, sea colour and visibility. It is useful for seeing current conditions, but it is not a substitute for an official marine or weather forecast.

Why the view moves

Only one rooftop camera is used. It rotates between selected views so the stream can show more of the bay than a fixed webcam. The long-term aim is intelligent camera direction that stays longer on strong scenes and leaves weak framing quickly.
